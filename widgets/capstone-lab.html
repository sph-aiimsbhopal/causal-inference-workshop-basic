Cooking Fuel and ARI: Build the Diagram

Draw your own causal diagram for a published NFHS analysis. Then check whether the authors' model could estimate the effect they wanted.

The study

Mondal and Paul used NFHS-4 (2015-16) to study 247,743 children under five. ARI meant cough with short, rapid or difficult breathing in the last two weeks, as reported by the mother. One logistic model held all of these together: biomass fuel, separate kitchen, household smoking, child's age and sex, birth order, mother's age and education, caste, religion, wealth, TB in the household and crowding.

They reported adjusted odds ratios of 1.10 for biomass fuel, 1.22 for no separate kitchen, and 1.32 for mothers with primary education against none. The authors call the education result surprising.

Mondal D, Paul P. PLoS ONE 2020;15(8):e0237611.

Things your group should argue about
  • The model did not include rural or urban residence, or state. Does your diagram need them?
  • One model, many odds ratios. Can the same adjustment set be right for fuel, for kitchen and for education?
  • Educated mothers may recognise and report fast breathing more often. Where would that sit in a diagram?
  • "Biomass" here includes kerosene, coal and dung. Is that one well-defined exposure?
