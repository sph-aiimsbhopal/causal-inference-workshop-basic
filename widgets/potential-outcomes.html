Potential Outcomes Lab

Every man has two possible futures: one if he smokes, one if he never does. We only ever see one of them.

Gajalakshmi and colleagues found that men in Tamil Nadu who smoked had 4.5 times the TB death rate of never smokers. They did not adjust for alcohol. Here is a made-up group of 100 men to show what that could mean. We built the true effect of smoking into it ourselves, so we can check any method against it. The risks are exaggerated so that you can see them.

Who smokes?

Four kinds of men

Rothman's sufficient-cause model says the same thing in another language: for some men, smoking completes a sufficient cause of TB death; for others the cause is already complete, or never will be.

Illustrative group of 100 men, constructed for teaching; only the 4.5 comes from the study (Gajalakshmi et al., Lancet 2003).
