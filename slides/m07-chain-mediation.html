The chain: a cause that works through something

Mothers who smoke in pregnancy have more infant deaths. How?

Smoking lowers birth weight.

Low birth weight raises the risk of infant death.

Low birth weight is a mediator: it lies on the causal path.

The chain: exposure, then mediator, then outcome. For now, suppose smoking acts only through birth weight.

Made-up numbers, chosen to land near the real US figures you meet in Module 8.

The death risk depends only on birth weight, not on smoking directly. So all of smoking's effect runs through low birth weight.

The total effect of smoking

Smokers: 0.14 × 4% + 0.86 × 0.4% = 0.560% + 0.344% = 0.904%

Non-smokers: 0.05 × 4% + 0.95 × 0.4% = 0.200% + 0.380% = 0.580%

Risk ratio = 0.904 / 0.580 = 1.56

Smoking raises infant deaths by 56%. That is the total effect, the one a mother or a programme cares about.

904 against 580 per 100,000 births. Work it in the workbook first.

Now "adjust for birth weight"

Low birth weight babies4%4%1.0
Normal weight babies0.4%0.4%1.0

Adjusted for birth weight, smoking looks harmless. Yet it raised deaths by 56%.

Stratifying on the mediator removes the very route the effect travels by. This is over-adjustment.

What happened, in the diagram

Green: a causal path, open. This carries the effect.

Adjusting for low birth weight blocks it (dashed). The effect is gone from the estimate, not from the babies.

Same blocking rule as Module 6: adjusting for a middle variable blocks the path. With a fork that is what we want; with a chain it is not.

How to spot a mediator

  • It is caused by the exposure.
  • It is usually measured after the exposure started.
  • Examples: cholesterol after starting hormones; CD4 count after starting ART; weight after joining a diet programme.

For the total effect, do not adjust, restrict, match or stratify on a mediator.

A rough but useful rule for beginners: be suspicious of any covariate measured after the exposure began.

But what if I want the direct effect?

Sometimes the question is: how much of the effect does not go through birth weight?

That is a different question, the direct effect. Simply adjusting for the mediator usually does not answer it, for a reason you will see in Module 8: the mediator can also be a collider.

Modern Epidemiology, 4th ed., chapter 27, Mediation Analysis.

Keep this short. Its job is to stop students concluding that adjusting for mediators is always wrong; it is wrong for the total effect, and too simple for the direct effect.

Your turn

ASHA home visits in pregnancy increase institutional delivery. Partly, visits raise the mother's knowledge of danger signs, and that knowledge leads to institutional delivery.

You want the total effect of home visits. Should you adjust for the mother's knowledge? Draw it.

No. Visits → Knowledge → Delivery is a chain: knowledge is a mediator. Adjusting would hide part of what the visits achieve.

A Community Medicine example to show the idea is not specific to birth weight.

What to carry forward

A chain carries the effect. The middle variable is a mediator.

Adjusting for a mediator removes part of the effect you want. In our numbers, 1.56 became 1.0.
