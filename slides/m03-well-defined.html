Module 3. Positivity and consistency in plain words: can everyone be exposed, and what exactly is 'the exposure'?

"If he had never smoked." Never smoked what?

Module 1 compared two futures: smokes, never smokes. But "smokes" hides many different lives.

  • Bidis or cigarettes?
  • Two a day, or twenty?
  • From age 15, or from age 40?
  • Still smoking, or quit five years ago?

Each of these is a different exposure, with a different effect.

This is the consistency condition in plain words: the exposure has to be defined well enough that "if exposed" means one thing.

Real figures from the paper. Place differs between the two rows too, so this is not a clean bidi against cigarette comparison; the point is only that "smoking" is several exposures.

So "the effect of smoking" depends on the mix

Suppose never smokers have a 1% risk, bidi smokers 6%, cigarette smokers 2%. Made-up numbers.

Town A, 70% of smokers use bidis: 0.7 × 6% + 0.3 × 2% = 4.8%. Risk ratio 4.8.

Town B, 30% use bidis: 0.3 × 6% + 0.7 × 2% = 3.2%. Risk ratio 3.2.

Same biology, same word "smoking", two answers. A vague exposure gives an answer that depends on who happened to be studied.

This is why an estimate for "ever smoked" may not transfer to a programme that targets bidis.

Consistency, in one sentence

The outcome we observe in an exposed person is the potential outcome under the version of the exposure he actually had.

For that link to mean anything, the exposure must be defined like an intervention: what would we actually change?

Modern Epidemiology, 4th ed., chapter 3. Hernán MA, Taubman SL. Int J Obes 2008;32 Suppl 3:S8-14.

Hernán and Taubman's paper on obesity is the classic: losing weight by exercise, by diet, by surgery or by illness are different interventions with different effects on death.

Some exposures are vaguer than they look

VagueCloser to an intervention
ObesityLosing 5 kg through a supervised diet over a year
Biomass fuelSwitching the household's main cooking fuel from wood or dung to LPG
ASHA contactThree or more ASHA home visits during pregnancy
Low educationCompleting secondary school instead of stopping after primary
Some exposures, like education or caste, cannot easily be imagined as interventions. You can still study them; just say what change you have in mind.

Positivity: could everyone have been exposed?

To compare exposed and unexposed within a group, the group must contain both.

No adjustment can produce an LPG effect for villages where nobody could use LPG. The data are silent there.

Made-up numbers.

Positivity fails structurally (impossible to be exposed) or by chance (too few exposed). Either way, say which population your answer applies to.

Your turn

A thesis question reads: "Effect of physical inactivity on hypertension among adults in an urban slum."

Rewrite the exposure as a change you could imagine making. Then name one group where positivity might fail.

For example: "Walking 30 minutes on at least 5 days a week, compared with less than 1 day." Positivity may fail among adults who cannot walk because of disability.

Accept any rewording that names a concrete change and a comparison.

What to carry forward

Define the exposure as a change you could make. "Ever smoked" is many exposures.

The estimate depends on the mix of versions in your study, so describe it.

Positivity: in any group where nobody is exposed, your data cannot say what exposure would do.
