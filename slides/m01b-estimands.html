Three groups, three averages

Average overMix, poor : goodEffect
Everyone (ATE)60 : 400.6 × (−1.5) + 0.4 × (−0.2) = −0.98
Those who joined (ATT)80 : 200.8 × (−1.5) + 0.2 × (−0.2) = −1.24
Those who did not (ATC)40 : 600.4 × (−1.5) + 0.6 × (−0.2) = −0.72

Same programme, three correct answers to three different questions.

ATT: effect among the treated. ATC: effect among the controls, the untreated. Here those who joined were mostly poor self-managers, who benefit most.

Which question does a programme officer ask?

  • "Should we roll this out to everyone?" The ATE.
  • "Was it worth it for those who came?" The ATT.
  • "What if we enrolled those who stayed away?" The ATC.

Matching on a propensity score usually estimates the ATT; weighting usually the ATE.

The last line previews methods that are outside this course but students will meet in papers.

A causal question has six parts

Population

In whom?

Intervention

What change?

Comparator

Against what?

Outcome

Measured how?

Time

When?

Measure

Difference or ratio?

Fix all six before choosing the analysis. Together they are called the estimand.

Close to PICO with time and effect measure added.

A precise question

Among adults with insulin-treated type 2 diabetes in urban Bhopal, what is the average effect of a structured self-management programme against usual care on HbA1c at six months, as a mean difference?

A study in rural Sehore could find a different effect. Both can be right: different populations, different estimands.

Adapted from Dr Ankur Joshi's lesson.

The Tamil Nadu 4.5 has an estimand too

Ever smokers against never smokers, TB death rate ratio 4.5.

  • Population: urban men aged 25 to 69 in Tamil Nadu, 1990s.
  • Exposure: ever smoking, any product. Comparator: never smoking.
  • Measure: a rate ratio.

It need not apply to women, to rural men, or to a ban on bidis alone.

Gajalakshmi V et al. Lancet 2003;362:507-15.

Spot the mismatch

"Among adults with type 2 diabetes, does a lifestyle programme against standard care reduce new cases of diabetes over six months?"

What is wrong with this question?

People who already have diabetes cannot newly develop it. Either change the population (adults with prediabetes, outcome new diabetes) or the outcome (adults with diabetes, outcome HbA1c).
