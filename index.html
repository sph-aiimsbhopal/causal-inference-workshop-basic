Pre-conference workshop for MD students

Causal Inference in Epidemiology: A Basic Workshop

Three hours on counterfactual thinking, causal diagrams, bias and choosing what to adjust for. Every exercise uses a published study.

By the end of the workshop you will be able to

  1. Explain causal effects using the counterfactual framework, and draw causal diagrams (DAGs).
  2. Identify and assess bias and confounding in observational studies using causal diagrams.
  3. Choose an adjustment set from a diagram, and frame and interpret causal research questions in Community Medicine.

How the three hours run

  1. Opening: the hormone therapy puzzle, and the before quiz
  2. Thinking causally: counterfactuals, exchangeability, sufficient causes. Exercise 1, smoking and TB deaths in Tamil Nadu
  3. Causal diagrams and the structure of bias: forks, chains, colliders. Exercises 2 and 3, hormone therapy and the birth weight paradox
  4. From diagram to adjustment: back-door paths, what not to adjust for, the Table 2 fallacy. Group exercise on cooking fuel and childhood ARI
  5. Report back, the after quiz, and where to go next
AIIMS Bhopal. Slides, labs and workbook built with Claude (Anthropic) as drafting and coding assistant, reviewed by the faculty. Studies are cited where they are used; the teaching populations in Labs 1 and 3 are constructed for teaching and labelled as such.
