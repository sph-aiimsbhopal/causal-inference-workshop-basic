Higher risk is not a bigger effect

Two groups, a treatment, risk of death. Made-up numbers.

World A

Low risk: 10% → 5%
High risk: 40% → 35%

−5 and −5 points. Same effect.

World B

Low risk: 10% → 8%
High risk: 40% → 20%

−2 and −20 points. Different effects.

When the effect differs between groups, we call it effect heterogeneity.

Adapted from Dr Ankur Joshi's lesson. Baseline risk is what happens without treatment; the effect is the change.

An average hides the spread

An average effect of −1 point can come from:

  • Everyone improving by about 1 point.
  • Some improving a lot, most not at all.
  • Most improving, a few harmed.

Module 1 showed this with three men: smoking killed Murugan, and made no difference to Selvam or Bala. The average tells you about the group, not the person in front of you.

Same ratio, ten times the benefit

Patient A

Risk 2% → 1%

Risk ratio 0.5

Difference 1 point
Treat 100 to prevent one death

Patient B

Risk 20% → 10%

Risk ratio 0.5

Difference 10 points
Treat 10 to prevent one death

The number needed to treat is 1 ÷ risk difference: 1 ÷ 0.01 = 100; 1 ÷ 0.10 = 10.

So: is there heterogeneity? It depends on the scale

GroupUntreatedTreatedRisk ratioRisk difference
120%10%0.5−10
260%30%0.5−30

The same on the ratio scale, different on the difference scale.

If a risk factor causes disease and baseline risks differ, the effect cannot be equal on both scales at once. Always say which scale you mean.

This is why "no interaction" is not a single statement. Rothman discusses effect-measure modification in chapter 5 and later chapters.

Which scale for public health?

  • Ratios are often similar across groups and travel well between studies.
  • Differences count cases prevented. They tell you where a programme does the most good.

For deciding whom to treat first, the difference usually matters most.

Classic illustration: Doll and Peto's British doctors, where smoking's ratio was far higher for lung cancer, but the excess deaths were larger for heart disease, because heart disease is common.

What to carry forward

An effect can differ between groups. A higher baseline risk alone does not prove that.

Same ratio, different differences: NNT 100 against 10.

Say which scale. "No heterogeneity" on one scale usually means heterogeneity on the other.
