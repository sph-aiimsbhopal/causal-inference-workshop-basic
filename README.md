# Causal Inference in Epidemiology: A Course for Beginners

A self-paced course for postgraduate students in Community Medicine on causal inference in observational epidemiology: potential outcomes, exchangeability, causal diagrams (DAGs), confounding, mediation, collider and selection bias, and choosing an adjustment set. It assumes only relative risks and 2 × 2 tables.

The course has sixteen short modules. Each has slides with one idea at a time and a worked number after every concept, a guided lab for phones, a short self-check, and fill-in workbook pages. Every example comes from a published study: the Nurses' Health Study and the Women's Health Initiative on hormone therapy, the Tamil Nadu case-control study of smoking and tuberculosis deaths, the birth weight paradox, and an NFHS-4 analysis of cooking fuel and childhood acute respiratory infection. Rothman's *Modern Epidemiology* (4th edition) is the anchor text.

The core modules also form the workshop path used at the pre-conference workshop of the 12th South Central Zone IAPSM Conference, Gwalior, November 2026. Printable workshop and course handbooks are included.
